What 96,497 Brazilian orders quietly teach us about late deliveries.

Olist runs the second-largest e-commerce marketplace in Brazil. Their public dataset is a goldmine — if you ask it the right questions. I asked ten.

The story in three lines

Late deliveries don't just annoy customers — they collapse the review score from 3.98 to 1.64. Once a review goes below 2 stars, the chance that customer ever comes back drops to almost zero. So I traced the lateness back to its source.

The bottleneck wasn't the seller. Sellers shipped fast (8 days median, even on late orders). The carriers held the packages for 25.6 days on average before delivering — almost 2x the on-time number. The fix isn't seller pressure. It's carrier accountability.

That single insight, surfaced from a 9-table SQL warehouse, redirects the entire customer-retention conversation away from "improve seller onboarding" and toward the real lever.

What's under the hood

  • 9 raw tables joined into a Kimball star schema (fact_orders + 5 dimensions).
  • 10 business questions answered in pure SQL — revenue concentration, state-level CLV, cohort retention, freight margin drag, seasonality.
  • 10 charts committed to the repo so reviewers can read the deck without running anything.
  • DuckDB as the embedded warehouse — runs locally in 12 seconds, portable to Snowflake/BigQuery/Redshift unchanged.
  • Synthetic data with deterministic seed — every chart regenerates byte-for-byte. No Kaggle download required.
Why this exists. The Olist dataset is the de-facto SQL benchmark for entry-level e-commerce analyst interviews in the US and Canada. This repo is my show, don't tell version of it — every query the interviewer might ask is already there, with the chart next to it.

What I learned the hard way

  • ~3% of orders are flagged canceled or unavailable — exclude from revenue, but keep for conversion math.
  • freight_value lives on the item table, not the order. Joining items to orders without aggregating first triples the freight totals — quietly.
  • Reviews can land months after delivery. Filter by review timestamp, not order timestamp, when you want fresh sentiment.
  • November 2017 has a Black Friday spike. Treat it as a normal month and your cohort retention baselines lie to you.
